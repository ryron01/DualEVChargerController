EV Charger Perfboard

70 × 90 mm perfboard, 26 × 31 holes · 37 wires · from the bench netlist

Parts go on the top. Every connection is an insulated wire on the bottom, soldered at both ends. The board is drawn as it sits in the enclosure, a quarter turn left from how it is printed: numbers 1–31 run left to right and letters A–Z run bottom to top. Holes use the printed names, letter then number, so A1 is the bottom-left hole. The bottom view is mirrored, so it matches the board when you flip it left to right. Click a wire in the lists to find it on both views.

Mains zone. The hatched area (numbers 1–9, letters A–F) carries 120 V from J1 to the HLK-PM01. The board has no fuse of its own, so feed J1 only from the inline 1 A fuse. Use insulated wire rated 300 V or more there, and run nothing else through it. Low-voltage parts and wires stay at least 10 mm from any mains hole.
Wire. 24–26 AWG insulated (silicone or PTFE) for low voltage; 20 AWG rated 300 V for mains. Wires may cross each other on the bottom; the routes shown avoid other nets' pads.
Diodes. The band (cathode) faces the PZEM header: D1 band at K28, D2 band at I28. Reversed diodes put 5 V on GPIO16 and block replies.
Modules on headers. The ESP32 and level shifter sit in female headers. Unused ESP32 pins still get a header socket; some wires pass under those joints on the bottom. The ESP32’s antenna connector ends up near the top-left of the module, a few millimetres from the board’s top edge.

Before the first power-up

  1. With nothing plugged in, check for no continuity between J1 L or N and any low-voltage point: +5V, GND, 3V3.
  2. Check no continuity between +5V and GND, or between 3V3 and GND.
  3. Check continuity from each header pin to its ESP32 socket, using the wire list.
  4. Feed J1 through the inline fuse from the GFCI bench feed, with the ESP32 and modules out, and measure 5 V at J4 VCC.
  5. Power off, fit the ESP32 and level shifter, power up and confirm the D1/D2 anode junction reads about 3.3 V.
