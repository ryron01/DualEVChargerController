Dual PZEM Bench Wiring

EV charger controller · bench build · rev 4 · Nov 2026

Two-outlet Level 1 controller on the bench. Each relay switches its outlet's hot, which threads through that channel's solid-core CT. Both PZEM-004T V4 modules take the same fused L and N, and they share one UART on the ESP32 through a Schottky diode-OR on TX. The low-voltage side runs from the HLK-PM01 over breadboard rails, and the 5 V LCD backpack talks to the ESP32 through a level shifter.

Check before applying power

  1. Confirm the PZEM terminal order with a meter.

    The V4 boards have no rear silkscreen. The reference diagram puts terminal 1 (nearest the VCC=3.3–5.0V edge) as N, 2 as L, and 3–4 as CT. With power off, terminals 3–4 should read a low resistance through the burden resistor and 1–2 should not. Your boards currently have red in terminal 1; if red means hot, swap it to terminal 2.

  2. Use the relay's NO contact, and check its silkscreen.

    NO keeps the outlets off when the ESP32 reboots or loses power. The NO/COM/NC order shown is typical for these modules, so read the screw-block label. Set the trigger jumper to H and confirm the module switches from a 3.3 V GPIO.

  3. Thread each hot through its CT before landing either end.

    The CTs are solid-core toroids. Run the hot only, from relay NO to the outlet's brass screw. Plug the CT leads into the PZEM before any load current flows.

  4. Size the wire by path.

    Feed, buses, relay COM/NO and outlets carry the charging load: 12 AWG to match the 20 A circuit. The taps behind the 1 A fuse feed only the PZEMs and the HLK-PM01, so 18 AWG is fine there.

  5. Route the LCD's I2C through the level shifter.

    The backpack pulls SDA and SCL up to its own 5 V supply, which is above the ESP32's 3.6 V limit, and the PCF8574 needs 3.5 V to see a logic high at 5 V. The shifter gives each side its own level: LV to 3V3, HV to 5V, GND shared, LV1/LV2 to G22/G23 and HV1/HV2 to the backpack's SDA/SCL. Power down before moving any I2C wire; an unpowered PCF8574 holds the bus low.

  6. Power the PZEM headers from 5 V.

    The RX optocoupler and its indicator LED drop about 3 V together. At 3.3 V the indicator blinks on every poll, but the module never answers.

  7. Check the diode-OR before connecting GPIO16.

    Each PZEM TX goes to the banded (cathode) end of its own Schottky; the anodes join at GPIO16 with 10 kΩ to 3V3. Powered up, the junction should read about 3.3 V. About 5 V means a diode is backwards. Tying the two TX pins together without diodes fails, because each module's 4.7 kΩ pull-up overpowers the other's optocoupler.

  8. Bring the PZEMs up one at a time.

    With AC live on terminals 1–2, confirm PZEM A answers alone at 0x01, then PZEM B alone at 0x02, before connecting both. With UART debug turned on (see the README), the first byte of each <<< reply in the ESPHome log is the address that answered.

5 V budget

The HLK-PM01 is rated 600 mA. The ESP32 peaks around 250 mA on Wi-Fi transmit, each SLA-05VDC coil draws about 90 mA, and the LCD backlight takes 20–30 mA. That totals roughly 460 mA with both relays on, which works but leaves little headroom for anything else on the rail.
